# Backstory
**Two shots rang out, piercing the thick silence of the frigid air of the desert night. The light from a campfire and the stars overhead were the only light in this barren wasteland. Joseph pushed the bodies aside and was about to start eating the meal they’d been preparing. 

“Why…” a voice groaned. 

Standing up slowly, Joseph took out his gun, pointed it at the C.C. belt buckle around the dying man’s waist, and shot him in the head two more times. He sat back down and began to eat. After finishing, he took an old, tarnished badge out of his pocket and gazed at it for a while. It both felt like just yesterday and a lifetime ago. 

___________

Joseph had always wanted to be a sheriff since he was a little boy. He practiced his shooting his entire life. Everyone told him he was the best they’d ever seen and he’d be the greatest sheriff in the desert. And he believed them. He became the sheriff, had the fastest draw in the country, and a family with his loving wife Lisa , and a beautiful baby boy Rowan. He’d never even come close to losing a duel with any trouble makers whether their bounty was 0 or 500 gp. A few years back, he figured he should start preparing to settle down. When Henrietta Willow,  a young woman he’d seen at the range approached him about becoming his deputy, he took her under his wing. She was almost as good a shot as him. Almost. He was the best, and he knew it. So, when his good friend, Terry Sycamore, warned him of the Clapton Crew’s crime spree, going from town to town taking down each sheriff in his path, he thought nothing of it.

When they came to town, he asked for their leader, Crie Clapton, and challenged him to a duel. If they won, the sheriff would be out of the way and they could do as they pleased, but if they lost, they’d have to leave town and never return. They began their paces.

Faster than anyone could blink, his gun left his holster

BANG!!

That's odd, he thought. Where did that come from? He hadn’t pulled the trigger yet. His eyes darted back and forth as he scanned the area. He saw Crie, still turning around, gun in hand, with a smug grin on his face. No smoke from there, so it wasn’t him. Next, he saw Terry, his hat covered his eyes and his mouth scrunched as he turned to look away. Then Henrietta, her face was pale, eyes wide, and mouth agape. He looked to his right. Smoke pouring from the barrel of one of the [[Clapton Crew]]’s guns. Then he looked down. His prized revolver, a gift from his late mother, was on the ground along with most of his fingers in a pool of blood streaming from where his right hand used to be. Like a cut that doesn’t hurt til you see it bleeding, the pain hit him all at once. His vision was blurry and pulsing. He dropped to his knees and grabbed arm to try and put some pressure on it. He looked up one more time. Through his fading vision, he saw Crie raise his gun and begin to chuckle. 

“Every town, every sheriff. You’re all the same.” He growled as he aimed right at Joseph’s head. At that moment, everything went dark.

Joseph awoke in a bed in the infirmary. Nothing fancy, but he had his own curtained off corner. His right arm was wrapped up and bloody. Looks like they had to amputate my hand, he thought. When he rubbed his face with his left, he felt a line of stitches along the left side of his mouth. He sat there in silence for a moment before he heard footsteps approaching. It was Terry. He wore a solemn expression that Joseph had seldom seen him with.  After he started explaining what had happened in the few days that he’d been here, he understood why he looked that way. First, he mentioned Henrietta had wanted to check on him, but she was busy running the crew out of town and helping with recovery efforts. After he went down, the Clapton Crew ran amok, looting homes, burning buildings, shooting people as they ran out. He’d heard about it happening to other towns, but now that the names of the injured and deceased were ones he knew, it made him more sick than he had been in his entire life. That wasn’t the worst news that came, however. Among the people killed were his wife and son. They were both trapped inside their house as it burned. He sat there in silence. 

___________

The drop of a burnt log from the fire broke his train of thought. After taking a bit more time to warm up, he put it out and continued on his way. Once he was done, the Clapton Crew would never hurt anyone again, and he’d return home a hero. The man who took down the depraved bastards who had trampled over so many people and ravaged their town. He would end them, or die trying.**
